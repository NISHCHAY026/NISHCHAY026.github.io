Research interests

  • AI-driven versus conventional methods in operations research: where learned models improve planning and inventory decisions, and where classical methods still win.
  • Pretrained time-series models used zero-shot for stocking decisions, and how to tell a gain that generalizes from one memorized in pretraining.
  • Decision-aligned evaluation of intermittent and lumpy demand: scoring a forecast by simulating the inventory policy it feeds.
  • Demand estimation from censored records, where what gets observed depends on the stock on hand.
  • Reproducible benchmarks on public data.

Working papers

Preprint 2026 · Version 2, October 7 · Sole author

Evaluation design decides the winner: horizon, leakage and metric effects in intermittent demand forecasting

Classical, gradient-boosted, deep and pretrained zero-shot forecasters on M5 (30,490 series) and UCI Online Retail II (4,675 series), all under one rolling-origin protocol. Changing only the evaluation design reorders the methods.

  • Scoring cumulative lead-time demand instead of one step ahead moves Croston's method on M5 from last of eight classical methods to fifth at a 13-week lead time.
  • Same-week price features in the initial Online Retail II setup leaked the target and carried 42 percent of the boosted model's gain. Once they were removed, the model fell from ahead of all eight classical methods to behind five of them.
  • Neither pretrained zero-shot model (Chronos-Bolt, TimesFM) beats the best trained model in any sparse demand class on either panel.

Preprint 2026 · Sole author

Evaluation pitfalls in intermittent-demand forecasting and inventory policy: a fleet-scale study of how far accuracy, calibration, and cost disagree

Eleven forecasters, from an operator's three deployed methods to gradient boosting and a pretrained zero-shot model, on 191,613 spare-parts items. Each forecaster is scored on point error and on simulated cost under an (s, S) inventory policy.

  • The per-item Kendall tau-b between error rank and cost rank is 0.35 on demand-active items, so accuracy predicts cost only moderately.
  • Safety stock taken from the predictive quantiles of count and boosted models did not beat a normal-approximation formula at matched fill rate, and the best-calibrated model lost the most.
